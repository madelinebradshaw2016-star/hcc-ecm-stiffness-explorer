How Matrix Stiffness Fuels Hepatocellular Carcinoma Metastasis

Most hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cases arise in cirrhotic livers characterized by extreme collagen crosslinking and matrix stiffening (rising from ~7 kPa to 53 kPa). This interactive application explores the mechanosensory signaling axis discovered by Wang et al. (2025): ECM rigidity drives tumor cell invasion through Integrin β1 mechanosensing, Plectin (~500 kDa cytolinker) upregulation, and accelerated F-actin stress fiber polymerization.

Translational Oncology

Mechanotransduction Pathway Mechanics & Clinical Horizons

From liver fibrosis elastography to anti-metastatic therapeutic targeting.

1 Fibrotic Liver Stiffening

Chronic hepatitis, NAFLD/MASH, and alcohol abuse activate hepatic stellate cells (HSCs), which secrete massive amounts of Collagen Type I (COL1A1) and Lysyl Oxidase (LOX). LOX enzymatically crosslinks collagen fibrils, transforming pliable liver parenchyma (~7 kPa) into stiff, cirrhotic tumor stroma (>53 kPa).

2 The Mechanosensory Relay

Stiff ECM exerts elevated isometric tension on cell-surface Integrin β1 heterodimers. Integrin β1 transmits mechanical cues inward, upregulating Plectin expression. Plectin, as a ~500 kDa cytolinker, physically bridges integrins to F-actin bundles and vimentin intermediate filaments, driving actin polymerization, stress fiber formation, and traction forces.

3 Therapeutic Implications

Wang et al. demonstrated that breaking any link in this axis—via Integrin β1 knockdown, Plectin silencing, or F-actin depolymerization—completely abrogates high stiffness-promoted cancer cell migration. This establishes Plectin and matrix-normalizing antifibrotic therapies as high-value avenues to prevent HCC metastasis.

Paper Details & Experimental Methodology

Full Citation

Wang, Z., Wang, W., Luo, Q. & Song, G. High matrix stiffness accelerates migration of hepatocellular carcinoma cells through the integrin β1-Plectin-F-actin axis. BMC Biology 23, 8 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-025-02113-1

Polyacrylamide (PA) Hydrogel Fabrication

PA hydrogels with tunable Young's modulus (7 kPa for physiological normal liver, 53 kPa for cirrhotic HCC tumor) were cast by varying acrylamide and bis-acrylamide ratios with ammonium persulfate (APS) and TEMED. Gels were covalently crosslinked with sulfo-SANPAH under 365 nm UV exposure and functionalized with 0.1 mg/mL rat-tail type I collagen (COL1).

Genetic Silencing Sequences

  • shPlectin-1: 5'-GCCUCUUCAAUGCCAUCAUTT-3'
  • shPlectin-2: 5'-GCCAGUACAUCAAGUUCAUTT-3' (Highest knockdown efficiency used for MHCC-97H)
  • si-Integrin β1: 5'-CCACAGCAGUUGGUUUUGCTT-3'
  • siNC (Non-targeting control): 5'-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3'

Microscopy & Wound Healing Assays

Ibidi Culture-Inserts (500 μm predefined gap) were placed onto PA hydrogels. Wound healing was captured at 0h and 12h, quantified in ImageJ. Immunofluorescence was imaged on a Leica TCS SP8 DIVE confocal microscope using Actin-Tracker Red-Rhodamine, rabbit anti-vimentin (ab92547), and DAPI.
